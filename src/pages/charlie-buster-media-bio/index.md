Charlie Buster is a British contemporary artist, muralist, creative consultant, and founder of the Grime Art movement.

Born and raised in Kent, England, Charlie’s artistic journey began almost as soon as he could hold a pencil. By the age of four he was already exploring graffiti, lettering, and visual storytelling, developing a fascination with the colours, textures, and messages that covered the walls, trains, estates, and underpasses of urban Britain.

What began as a childhood obsession evolved into a professional career spanning more than two decades.

In 2008, Charlie joined Graffiti Kings London, one of the world’s leading street art and mural production companies. Over the following decade he developed from an in-house artist into a senior creative figure working across murals, public art, commercial commissions, workshops, brand activations, marketing, partnerships, and large-scale creative projects for globally recognised organisations.

Throughout his career, Charlie has produced artwork for musicians, celebrities, luxury brands, charities, hospitality groups, public institutions, and private collectors. His portfolio includes projects connected with Gordon Ramsay’s Lucky Cat restaurants, Aston Merrygold, Central Cee, Giggs, Kidd Keo, Big Zuu, Not3s, Yxng Bane, Knucks, Furla, Adidas, Nike, EOS USA, and numerous other cultural and commercial organisations.

One of his most notable commissions involved hand-painting 300 individual Lucky Cat sculptures for Gordon Ramsay’s flagship Lucky Cat restaurant concept. The project later became part of the wider story featured in the Netflix documentary series “Being Gordon Ramsay.”

Alongside commercial success, Charlie has remained deeply connected to public art and community engagement. He has delivered workshops, murals, educational programmes, youth outreach projects, and community-led initiatives across schools, community centres, youth clubs, charities, and rehabilitation programmes throughout the United Kingdom, helping thousands of people engage with creativity through art.

At the centre of Charlie’s practice is Grime Art, a term he began developing in 2011 to describe a distinctly British visual art movement inspired by the raw energy of graffiti, street culture, music, fashion, urban decay, aspiration, and contemporary life.

Rather than viewing graffiti as something confined to walls and public spaces, Charlie reimagines its layered textures, rebellious spirit, and cultural significance through collectible contemporary artworks, murals, sculptures, custom apparel, and large-scale installations.

Today, Charlie Buster’s work sits at the intersection of street culture and fine art. His paintings combine graffiti influences, portraiture, typography, symbolism, popular culture, and luxury aesthetics to create visually striking works that are both culturally relevant and highly collectible.

As the founder of Grime Art, Charlie continues to build a body of work that challenges traditional boundaries between the street and the gallery while documenting contemporary British culture through a uniquely authentic artistic voice.
